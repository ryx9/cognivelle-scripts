# Cold-Calling Script: Dental Practices & Clinics

> **Track A**: Professional Website & Digital Booking System  
> Built strictly adhering to [`skill.md`](../skill.md) specifications.

---

## Prospect Context

* **Business:** [Dental Practice / Clinic Name]
* **Industry:** Dentistry / Dental Care
* **Location:** [City, State]
* **Website:** [No dedicated website / Website present without self-serve booking]
* **Booking System:** [Phone call only during office hours / General contact form]
* **Relevant Observation:** New or existing patients cannot view clear procedure information or request/schedule appointments outside office hours.

---

## Objective

Secure permission for a 10-minute walkthrough/demo showing a tailored dental website and seamless appointment scheduling flow for their clinic.

---

## Gatekeeper Strategy (Front Desk / Reception)

**Salesperson:**
> "Hi, good morning. My name is [Rep] with [Company]. I'm trying to reach whoever handles the website or patient scheduling for [Practice Name]—would that be the practice manager or Dr. [Last Name]?"

*(If asked what it is regarding:)*
> "It's about your website and online patient appointment requests. I wanted to share a quick observation with whoever manages that."

---

## Primary Script

### 1. Opening & Reason for Call
**Salesperson:**
> "Hi [Doctor / Practice Manager Name], this is [Rep] with [Company]. I know I'm calling out of the blue, but the reason for my call is we build clean, modern websites with online booking systems specifically for dental practices."

### 2. Specific Observation (The Hook)
**Salesperson:**
> "I came across [Practice Name] and noticed you have great reviews in [City], but when patients find you online after hours, there isn't an easy way for them to see your treatment options and request an appointment without calling the office directly."

### 3. Light Discovery Question
**Salesperson:**
> "How are you currently handling appointment bookings for patients who find you outside regular office hours?"

---

## Dialogue & Qualification Flow

### Branch A: "They usually just call our front desk or leave a voicemail."
**Salesperson:**
> "That's very common. What we do is give your practice a clean, mobile-friendly website where new patients can see your services—like cleanings, whitening, or cosmetic work—and immediately choose a time to request an appointment. It saves your front desk from phone tag and catches patients when they're actively searching at night."
> 
> "Would you be open to a quick 10-minute walkthrough this week to see how that would look for [Practice Name]?"

### Branch B: "We already have a website."
**Salesperson:**
> "Got it. Are you completely happy with how it's performing, or is there anything about it you'd want to improve?"
> 
> *(If happy with website:)*  
> "Understood. The main reason I reached out wasn't to replace a site that's working, but specifically to check if patients can seamlessly schedule visits right from it. Is online booking something you've considered adding?"

### Branch C: "We already have an online booking system / patient portal."
**Salesperson:**
> "Understood. Are you happy with how easy it is for new patients to use, or does your staff still end up taking most bookings manually over the phone?"
> 
> *(If they are satisfied:)*  
> "That makes total sense. If your current workflow is working smoothly, I wouldn't suggest changing it just for the sake of it. Thanks for letting me know, and have a great rest of your day."
> 
> *(If they report friction/high cost/clunky setup:)*  
> "Makes sense—a lot of patient portals can be difficult for first-time visitors to navigate on their phones. We keep the front-facing booking experience lightweight so patients actually complete it. Would it be worth taking 10 minutes to look at a simpler setup?"

---

## Objection Branches

### If they say "I'm not interested."
**Salesperson:**
> "Totally fair. Just so I don't waste your time—is it the online booking concept itself, or is this just not a priority right now?"
> 
> *(If not a priority:)*  
> "Completely understand. Thanks for your time today."

### If they ask "How much does it cost?"
**Salesperson:**
> "It depends mainly on the scope of the website and how your appointment calendar is structured. Generally, it's a straightforward one-time setup without locking you into expensive proprietary software contracts. If you have 10 minutes, I can show you a live example and give you an exact quote based on what you actually need."

### If they say "Just send me information / email me."
**Salesperson:**
> "I'd be glad to. Just so I send something genuinely relevant rather than a generic brochure, are you more interested in modernizing the website design or adding direct online appointment scheduling for patients?"
> 
> *(After response:)*  
> "Great, what's the best email address to send that over to Dr. [Last Name] / you?"

### If they say "I'm busy right now."
**Salesperson:**
> "I completely respect that—I'll keep it to ten seconds. We build modern websites with online appointment scheduling for dental practices so new patients can book without tying up your phone lines. Would Thursday morning or Friday afternoon work better for a brief 5-minute call?"

### If the prospect is distracted or there is heavy background noise:
**Salesperson:**
> "It sounds like you have a lot going on in the background right now. I don't want to add to the chaos—should I reschedule the call to a later time, or do you have a brief 30 seconds?"

### If the prospect is angry or annoyed by the call:
**Salesperson:**
> "I sincerely apologize for the interruption, I know unexpected calls are frustrating when you're running a practice. I will take your number off our list immediately. Have a great rest of your day."
*(End the call gracefully without any pressure.)*

### If they ask "Where are you based?"
**Salesperson:**
> "We're based in the United States, and we have our engineering and development team based in Pakistan. That allows us to build fully custom, high-quality websites and booking workflows at a very accessible price point for local practices."
